01 / Framework

Green Hydrogen, Black Numbers

Hydrogen projects rarely fail on technology alone. They fail on delivered cost, on offtake that isn't firmly guaranteed, on infrastructure beyond the fence that the project has to negotiate access to, and on permitting and utility-supply delays that can run years over. I published a five-test screen for separating the projects that reach black numbers from the ones that do not, and I hold my own work to the same five tests I'd apply to anyone else's.

The framework is free to use and reproduce under a Creative Commons licence. It was first presented at CERAWeek in Houston in March 2026.

03 / Fellowship

Churchill Fellow 2026

I hold a 2026 Churchill Fellowship in governance and public policy for Trust by Design: Making Clean Energy Welcome in Every Community. The subject is delay. The transition to net zero requires an estimated $3.5 trillion of annual capital spending, and that figure assumes the money is deployed efficiently, which it rarely is. Solar farms and onshore wind projects stall for years, and are sometimes killed outright, by community and regulatory conflict.

The Fellowship takes me to Chile, Norway and Japan to study how the best project teams manage that conflict and hold schedule. Three very different political cultures and operating environments, which is the reason for choosing them. The output will be a public benchmarking report on reducing delay in green infrastructure.
