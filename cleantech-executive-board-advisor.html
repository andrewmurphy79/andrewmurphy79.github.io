Advisory, board and non-executive

What I take on

Four kinds of mandate, and the conditions under which each is worth doing.

01 / Diligence

Technical and commercial diligence for investors

Hydrogen and clean energy assets are hard to underwrite because technical risk, offtake risk and infrastructure risk are entangled and usually presented separately. I read the case as one system and report the failure modes in the order they are likely to bite, using the five tests.

The output is short. What has to be true for this to work, which of those things is currently unevidenced, and what the sponsor would have to produce to close each gap. Most investors read the second list first.

02 / Gates

Project gate review for operators and developers

Most project failures are visible well before they are acknowledged, and they are easiest to see at a gate, by someone with no stake in the answer. I take that role: reviewing whether the case approved at the last gate still holds, and which assumptions carried forward have quietly become commitments.

I have defended projects at gates as well as reviewed them, at Shell, at bp and at Watts Water. A review written without understanding the pressure on a project team produces findings nobody acts on.

03 / Board

Board and advisory board seats

I chaired the advisory board of the 800 MW Southern Green Hydrogen programme, advising the joint venture CEOs on investment timing, sequencing and route to market, and I served on the Board of Directors of the US Hydrogen and Fuel Cell Partnership. I advised the Petronas board on a $1.3bn hydrogen and ammonia export facility.

What I bring to a board is translation in both directions: telling a technical team what the board actually has to decide, and telling a board what the engineering position is behind the summary slide. I hold a small number of these at a time and I declare conflicts early. If I hold a position that competes with yours, you will hear it before the first meeting.

04 / Standards

Safety, standards and consent

I chair Energy Institute working groups on hydrogen safety distances and hydrogen refuelling guidance, and I sit on BSI GSE/6 and participate in ISO/TC 197. Organisations moving into hydrogen consistently underestimate the competence gap. Natural gas and industrial gas experience transfers only partially, and the training and assurance regime is a cost line that appears late and large.

I advise on which standards apply, how to build a competence framework an insurer and a regulator will both accept, and how to assemble a separation-distance evidence pack that survives planning. My Churchill Fellowship work on reducing delay in green infrastructure feeds directly into this.

05 / Fit

When I am the wrong person

I am the wrong choice if you want a report that supports a decision already taken. I do not write fundraising narrative, and I will not put my name to a market forecast I would decline to defend on a panel. If a project needs an advocate, there are people who do that work well and I am happy to name a few.

Board, advisory, diligence and speaking enquiries. Tell me the decision you are facing and the date it has to be made by.
